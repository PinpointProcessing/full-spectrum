# Warehouse Yard — squad tactics prototype

Combat demo for Bryan's next game after Ashvale: modern squad tactics (Full Spectrum Warrior squad control + Aliens: Dark Descent look and campaign feel) with base building and async PvP planned later. This demo only tests the battle layer.

## The game idea (long term)
- Main HQ: level up, research weapons, build defenses, store vehicles, collect resources. Modern setting, human vs human.
- Missions: overlay of enemy territory, scouting options, pick the first target, take it, then push to the next outpost/checkpoint. Captured areas become sub-bases (recruit locals, repair gear, heal the wounded, build makeshift tech). Clear the region, upgrade HQ, continue the war.
- Later: async online raids. Players build base defenses, and their forces defend on their own when attacked.
- Visual target: close to Aliens: Dark Descent.

## The demo (warehouse-yard.html)
`warehouse-yard.html` is the page shell (markup only). Three.js r128 from cdnjs (global `THREE`) renders the 3D world; a 2D canvas overlay on top draws the HUD bits (hp bars, hit %, previews, tracers, rain). No build step: open the HTML directly or serve the folder statically.

### Decisions made (keep unless Bryan changes them)
- Real-time with slow-mo (quarter speed) while giving orders, plus Pause. Not turn-based.
- Squad is the main unit, split into two 2-man fire teams. Individuals are for special moves (flank, grenade, revive).
- Group move = two taps: first tap previews ghost positions (green = in cover, red = exposed, from known enemies) and the route each man will take, tap the marker again to go. Single soldier moves on one tap.
- Vaulting through windows and over sandbags and barriers (Bryan: windows when it's the quickest or safest way in; sandbags: "jump over sandbags or walk through from either side"): they're links in the squad's pathfinding (`VAULTABLE`, `buildVaultLinks`, `vaultLinks`, `WINDOWS` in pathfinding.js, which despite the name holds every vaultable object: free cells straight across one where a body fits over it), costing their length plus `VAULT_EXTRA` (4.5 cells ≈ the climb's extra time). The climb: within `VAULT_HALF` (13) of the window's middle he moves at `VAULT_SPEED` (20/s) with a hop of `VAULT_H` (10) over the sill, leaning forward, card status "Vaulting", no firing; about 1.3 s. Waypoints after a vault carry `.vault`; route lines and previews draw a little arc over the window (`vaultMark` in overlay.js). The windows' glass is broken out (jagged bits left). The enemy doesn't vault (its moves use `FOOT`). Usable windows: both warehouse windows.
- Safest routes: the squad's moves (`SQUAD` nav, the default) also try a route that keeps out of what known enemies (seen, at their last known spots) would actually spot them in: an unaware one's view cone (±62°, 300, the red wedge on the map) or within 80; an alerted one all round within 380, counting less further off (Bryan found the first version, any line of sight within 38 m, made funny detours near the canal). Sight is a quick check on a grid of the sight-blocking objects (`gridSight`): each cell costs ×(1+n), n capped at 3 (machine guns count 1.5), vaults included. That route is taken if it's at most 1.3× the quickest + 30 (`SAFE_DETOUR`). A tap on water (canal, dock) means the bank on the soldiers' side (`nearBank` in combat.js), so nobody gets sent across. Before any enemy is seen, routes are simply the quickest. Drawn routes fill their gaps without detours (`TRAIL`).
- Where soldiers stand (`makeAssign`, reworked after Bryan's complaints about far-off cover and runners): spots within 14 of the tap for one soldier, 32 for two, 45 for three or four, and in line of sight of the tap (same side of a wall). Candidates are cover points plus open slots in a line across the threat direction (back row penalised). The whole group's arrangement is chosen together: cover (×2) + sees enemy (0.3) − distance from tap/12 − walk/150 − spread beyond 30, and health counts (Bryan: the healthier man goes in front; a weak soldier at a wall's edge kept going down and being revived): each spot's exposure (sees or peeks at the enemy: 1, not fully covered: 1 more) × (his HP − the group's average)/50, and −0.3 more per exposure for anyone under 45 HP in a group, so the healthiest takes the corner. Tapping a wall/container/crate/sandbag in the 3D view targets the spot just in front of the face you tapped (`tapTarget` in scene.js, using `objHeight` from map.js), not the ground hidden behind it.
- Drawn routes for independent soldiers (Bryan, inspired by mobile squad games): drag from a soldier to draw his route. He follows the line's shape; stretches through objects are routed round (`routeFromTrail` in combat.js, dropping a dead-end bit of line if that saves 15%+ locally); the end settles on cover within 18. Quarter speed while drawing, works while paused, map edge-scrolls while drawing.
- Sneak/run toggle (Bryan's design): button bottom right (`#gaitBtn`, `toggleGait`) shows the selected soldiers' gait (AUTO SNEAK / AUTO RUN). Pressing flips them to the other gait (amber, MANUAL), pressing again returns to auto. A soldier goes back to auto when he's selected again after not being selected (`select()` in input.js; always change the selection through it). Key R.
- Weapons tight while undetected: before contact the squad only fires at a hostile you tapped (or via Suppress/grenade). Striking first is an ambush: enemies need 1.4–2 s to react and the squad gets +0.15 hit chance for 1.5 s (`alertAll(msg,true)`, `ambushing()`). The hit pill shows "· ambush" while undetected. After contact, everyone fires freely.
- Soldiers act on their own to survive: return fire, shift to cover within 50 if exposed ~2 s, but never wander off on a plan of their own. Player orders lock out auto-moves for 4 s.
- Angled 3D camera, from the south by default (pitch 60° zoomed out → 46° zoomed in), rotatable by twisting. Bryan liked this far more than top-down. Screen-relative things (edge-scroll, labels placed below a point via `below()`) must account for `yaw`.
- Hit chance shown per shot as "64% · open / cover / heavy", XCOM style: best of the selected soldiers on that enemy; adds "· peek" if the shot needs a lean and "· ambush" while undetected; green ≥60 %, amber ≥35 %, red below; grey "no line" if nobody selected can shoot him.

### Testing note
Headless browsers here have no GPU, so WebGL runs in software at ~3 fps: fine for screenshots and checking layout, useless for judging pacing. For local tests without network, install `three@0.128.0` from npm and serve `build/three.min.js` for the CDN URL (e.g. Playwright `page.route`), or swap the CDN tag for a local copy. Because the game state is global, tests can drive it straight from `page.evaluate` (`update(1/60)`, `tap(...)`, `units`), and seeding `Math.random` makes runs repeatable. To test inside the office building, store a 'dco' save (`warehouseYard.save.v3`, `cleared:[0,1,2,3]`) and call `continueGame()`, put the squad in `DC_ENTER` and `interiorTap(245,-410)` (or store a 'dci' save: Continue goes straight in); the fade runs on real-time timeouts and `requestAnimationFrame`, so wait about a second with the frame loop running. Things inside are driven through `ACTS` (find one by id: 'computer', 'card', 'stairs-GFB1', 'lift-GF', 'liftdoor', 'cell-C1', 'cutdown') and `orderUse(a)` with soldiers selected; the story's state is in `office`. Level 1: `startLevel1()` (or click `#l1Btn`); its state is in `l1` and `L1` (guards in `L1.fence`, `L1.gate`, `L1.yard`), the tutorial in `tut` (`tut.i` is the boot camp's step; `tut.i=TUT_STEPS.length` goes straight to the tips). Its lock holds back taps, clicks and `tap()` calls while a step or tip is up: a test either does what it asks, clicks `#tut` for a notice, or sets `tut.on=false`. Detection by team: `teamSeen`, `caught(u)`, `startContact(msg,amb,who)` (setting `contact=true` by hand, with no team marked, counts as everyone caught); a radio call alerts every hostile outdoors, so set `e.called=true` on the radio men to keep a test's map quiet. Don't name a test helper `place` (audio.js has a global of that name).
