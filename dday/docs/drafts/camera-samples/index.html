D-Day · after Version 174

Four cameras, one advance

The same run up the beach map, filmed by four cameras at the same moments. Real game footage on a phone held upright: Alpha (blue rings) runs the route by itself, Bravo follows, and nobody gets hurt, so you only see what the cameras do. There are three stretches, cut between:

  • 1 · The sand: straight north to the wire
  • 2 · The way up: the route turns north-west
  • 3 · The top: it turns east to the barracks

All four at once

Watch the way up and the top: that's where the route turns and the cameras part ways.

A TodayB Set view per section C Turns with the routeD Leader’s shoulder

A Today

Behind the man furthest ahead, always facing north. A drag of the map stops it following, and it only turns when you twist it.

Good
Close to the men; nothing new to learn.
Not so good
When the route turns, the way ahead runs off the side of the screen. On top, the barracks wall fills the view while the machine gun is off to the right.

B A set view for each section

Each stretch of the map has a view framed by hand. When the squad reaches the next stretch, the camera glides there (on the way up it glides from the bluff to the draw).

Good
You see the whole stretch and its guns; the camera never moves while you aim.
Not so good
Your men are small on a phone, and every big map needs its views set by hand.

C Turns with the route My pick

Today’s camera, a little higher, turned to face the route about 18 m ahead of the lead man. It turns by itself as the route turns. Drag, pinch and twist would still work, and it would ease back.

Good
The way ahead is always up the screen, with the men as close as today.
Not so good
The map turns under you on the bends, and it needs the route on each map (the beach and village have one).

D Over the leader’s shoulder

Low behind one man, looking where the squad is going, like Full Spectrum Warrior. D-Day has no squad leader yet, so here it rides with the man at the back.

Good
Close and dramatic; you feel you’re with them.
Not so good
The nearest men hide what’s ahead, and you see little to the sides on a phone held upright.
